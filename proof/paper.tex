\begin{abstract}
We present a computer-assisted approach to making the lower bound
$L(1,\mathrm{sym}^2 f) \geq c/\log N$ (Goldfeld--Hoffstein--Lieman 1994) effective.
The paper has two components: (1) a rigorous re-derivation of the local Euler factor
factorization via the SU(2) Clebsch--Gordan identity; (2) a research programme
toward the global residue positivity input and the explicit constant.
For the Ramanujan $\Delta$ function ($N=1$), Theorem~F-3 certifies
$L(1,\mathrm{sym}^2\Delta) \in [0.63179293, 0.63179298]$ (width $4.6\times10^{-8}$).
The general explicit constant $c_{\mathrm{eff}}$ remains an open obligation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

For a normalized primitive holomorphic Hecke eigenform $f\in S_k(N)$, the symmetric
square $L$-function $L(s,\mathrm{sym}^2 f)$ is a degree-3 $L$-function. Its value at $s=1$
controls: Quantum Unique Ergodicity rates (Holowinsky--Soundararajan 2010),
Ramanujan coefficient bounds, and Sato--Tate eigenvalue distribution.

The known lower bound $L(1,\mathrm{sym}^2 f) \geq c/\log N$ (Goldfeld--Hoffstein--Lieman 1994)
is \emph{ineffective}: the constant $c>0$ is not computable from the proof.

\textbf{Our approach.}
(1) Exact derivation of the Rankin--Selberg Euler factor via Clebsch--Gordan identity.
(2) A planned pointwise global residue positivity input, with the source-level
    adjoint and bad-local-factor gaps, together with the quantitative
    exceptional-zero alternative, left as explicit obligations.
(3) A planned computer-assisted Arb certificate for the $\Delta$ function.
(4) Research programme toward explicit $c_{\mathrm{eff}}$ via explicit zero-free region.

\section{Global Residue Positivity \texttt{[OBL]}}

The desired statement in \texttt{proof/02-global-residue.tex} is:
\[
  \mathrm{Res}_{s=1}\langle 1, \mathcal{T}_{s,\Phi}^{\mathrm{JS}}[W_f\otimes W_{\bar f}]\rangle
  = \hat\Phi(0)\cdot\kappa_F\cdot L(1,\pi,\mathrm{Ad})\cdot\|W_f\|^2 > 0.
\]
It remains open because the exact adjoint nonvanishing input and the positivity
of every bad local correction factor have not been proved.

\section{Instance Certification: Ramanujan $\Delta$ Function \texttt{[THM]}}

\begin{theorem}[F-3]\label{thm:f3}
$L(1,\mathrm{sym}^2\Delta) \in [0.63179293,\, 0.63179298]$ (width $4.6\times10^{-8}$).
\end{theorem}

Proved via AFE with N=3000 terms, 256-bit Arb precision, and two-point truncation
error bound.  Certificate: \texttt{witness/single\_point\_certificate.json}.
The former claimed interval $[2.405,2.407]$ is retracted (wrong RS identity).
Zero-free region: $L(s,\mathrm{sym}^2\Delta) \neq 0$ for $\sigma \in [0.6,1]$, $|t| \leq 20$.
